# The Counting Dark

### A Chronicle of the Hargate Delve — Sessions I–IV

*As recorded by the Dungeon Master, from the annals of Hebach*

---

## Dramatis Personae

**Baelson** — *Human Paladin of St. Cuthbert. Played by Benjamin Boulanger.* The self-styled **Avenging Night Angel of St. Cuthbert**, a grim zealot in chain mail who came to Hargate hunting an imagined criminal underground. Forged from the armored excess of nineties comic books, Baelson does not investigate so much as *pursue*, and his answer to nearly every question is justice — loudly, and at the end of a mace. "My greatest nightmare," he once declared without a trace of irony, "is a world of injustice."

**Vlix Boggs** — *Aasimar Warlock of the Queen of Worlds. Played by Brian Starke.* Once a frumpy, bespectacled tavern orphan of Hargate, Vlix was remade in a single night by trickster fae — teal-eyed, beautiful, sassy as a southern summer, and armed with an Eldritch Blast she barely understands. She carries the *Ledger of Souls*, a patron-gifted tome, and a pomegranate bond that ties her soul to both life and death: radiant in the bright months, necrotic when the dark ones come. Her charisma is her entire character sheet. She knows it. "Well, first of all, my eyes are up here, sugar."

**Randælfine Carter** — *Drow Warlock of the Great Old One. Played by Cotter Kelsey.* A mad male drow, criminal-born, imprisoned and tortured by the matriarchy of the deep cities, who crawled out of the dungeons and into the embrace of Cthulhu. His grand design — insofar as madness has design — is to aim the Great Old One's hunger at Lolth herself and watch the matriarchy burn. He speaks in a syllabic giggle that is almost a language, dances forever at the border where torchlight dies into shadow, and whispers uninvited into the minds of anyone within thirty feet. He is delighted by everything you fear.

**Pim** — *Svirfneblin Rogue. Played by Gregory Jones-Katz (from Session III).* A deep gnome guide of proper manners and quiet prestige among her people, curious about the strange "tops" from the surface world. She writes in the secret stone-script of the svirfneblin, materializes out of shadows with a courteous bow, and has become dangerously fascinated with Baelson's intensity of purpose. When the drug called brain cane enters her small body, Pim becomes **Pimp** — faster, louder, and utterly convinced of her own invincibility.

**The Company They Keep:**

- **Brother Hethrin & Sister Maugra** — dwarven clerics of the **Vault of Dumathoin**, who hired the party to bring home a lost holy man.  
- **Cororic** — a cleric of the Vault who vanished into the deep a generation ago. Something down there now calls to the townsfolk in his voice.  
- **Vesque** — svirfneblin guide, first to die.  
- **Glul & Lib** — kuo-toa cultists of unusual sanity. One of them remains.  
- **Nyx** — a goblin entrepreneur of the mushroom trade, much put-upon.  
- **Gri the Asht-Chewer** — goblin shaman, cooker of brain cane, at large.  
- **Ulu** — kuo-toa choir master of the defiled cathedral.  
- **The Witness** — the thing behind the eye. It is counting.

## Session I — *It's Not a Wishing Well*

*July 5th*

Hargate is a fading mountain town in the realm of Hebach, its glory days as host of the **Goat Goat Festival** — a yearly tournament to crown the finest goat in all the world — long behind it. Three strangers took rooms in the same inn: a paladin convinced the festival was a front for a criminal underworld, a newly-transformed tavern girl treating herself to a stay in the establishment that had just employed her, and a hooded drow who had followed a week of signs and portents up from the deep.

That night, all three dreamed the same dream.

> *"You feel a faint wrong light behind your eyes, the color of nothing — and under it a voice, patient, kind, and very old. It is counting. It reaches a number. It pauses. And in that pause, you understand, the way you only understand things in dreams: it is counting people. And it has not yet counted you."*

They woke to screaming in the street. A gnome mother wailed for her boy **Pang**, who had walked off into the delving in his nightshirt — the latest in a plague of dream-driven disappearances. Randælfine glided to her side with a silken lie ("I am a healer, a cleric"), Baelson attempted to slap the panic out of her by sheer intimidation, and before the situation could deteriorate further, two dwarves in the vestments of the Vault of Dumathoin inserted themselves between the party and the widow.

Brother Hethrin and Sister Maugra had a proposition. A generation ago, a holy man of their order — **Cororic** — vanished into the Underdark below Hargate. Now something down there was calling townsfolk in his voice. They wished to mount an expedition and bring him home, kindly. The party's motives aligned imperfectly but sufficiently: Baelson smelled injustice, Vlix wanted out of town, and Randælfine wanted to be present when the nightmares arrived. In the tavern where Vlix had grown up, they hired two svirfneblin guides, **Vesque** and young **Pim**, and descended.

At the threshold of the dark, Randælfine offered the party his benediction:

> **Randælfine:** "Conjure your greatest nightmare and hold fast to it — for it shall be the most friendly thing we encounter."  
>   
> **Baelson:** "My greatest nightmare is a world of injustice."  
>   
> **Randælfine:** "...That we will find."

The tunnels turned magnetic. Weapons tugged toward the walls, compasses were useless, and Pim confessed she'd lost the way. When the path forked, Randælfine's telepathic whispers set the dwarves against the gnomes, and the party followed the clerics — Vlix sealing the fractured peace by charming Sister Maugra with a *Friends* spell and a touch of makeup. ("Sometimes you catch more flies with honey.")

Then the walls stood up. Two headless golems of magnetite and scrap tore themselves free of the tunnel and attacked. It was an ugly, grinding fight: Baelson's mace struck home and *stuck fast* to the first golem's magnetic hide, leaving the Night Angel wrestling his own weapon while Sister Maugra was battered to the ground with internal injuries. Vlix's Witch Bolt proved the weapon of the day, its sustained arc disrupting the creatures' humming cores, until Brother Hethrin's Guiding Bolt collapsed the first golem into a rain of scrap. The second died worse — it stopped humming, reversed polarity, and *detonated*, peppering Baelson with shrapnel.

In the debris they found Cororic's compass, needle spinning uselessly, and a metal-bound notebook written in the scratch-script of the kuo-toa. Sister Maugra translated a single phrase, repeated over and over:

> *"It's not a wishing well. It's a question."*

Deeper in, they found Vesque — dead, opened by claws no person possesses, while a hopeful gnomish voice called *"I found the way\!"* from ahead. Vlix read the tracks: beasts only. No gnome had come this way. The voice was a lure.

Two pale, clawed things burst from the dark. Baelson stepped forward to meet them.

*And somewhere below, something was still counting.*

## Session II — *The Vault of Dumathoin*

*July 19th*

The creatures were eyeless, four-limbed, ape-quick horrors — pale as drowned flesh, all claw and hiss. The party would come to call them **miscallers**, for what they could do with a voice. A third lurked at the edge of Baelson's torchlight; only he saw it peer around the corner.

Randælfine opened the battle in signature style, his Eldritch Blast a wave of blobby, clear-eyed heads erupting endlessly from one another's screaming mouths. Vlix opened hers with an insult:

> **Vlix:** "Honey, you got a face only a toilet could love."

The miscaller, tragically, had no problems with toilets, and shrugged off the Vicious Mockery. Then the party heard *Pim* — screaming in agony from down the hallway behind them. Every one of them saw through it at once: ventriloquism, thrown from the throat of the eyeless thing before them. Randælfine felt only professional respect. *Game recognizes game.*

The fight turned farcical and brutal by turns. Baelson dropped his torch to swing — "These tunnels are a cesspool, and I'm here to clean them\!" — missed catastrophically, and stomped out his own light, plunging himself into darkness. Vlix, incensed at being ignored ("Didn't you hear me? I called you *ugly*"), landed a critical Eldritch Blast that dropped a miscaller to one knee; Brother Hethrin cracked its spine as it fled. Baelson relit his torch as a bonus action, hurled himself into a flying football tackle, and grappled the last one to the ground, where Sister Maugra's Sacred Flame descended and *smited* it dead in his arms. The gods, it seems, do not look kindly on those who touch Sister Maugra.

One miscaller escaped into the dark. Randælfine leaned into Baelson's mind and stoked the furnace:

> **Randælfine** *(telepathically)*: "Pursue the unjust. *Pursue.*"  
>   
> **Benjamin, observing his own creation:** "Just keep shoveling coal into the madness."

A charisma check and a randomly-rolled d8 later, Baelson was charging blind down a southeasterly tunnel, torch held high, bellowing — "Stand down and face the Avenging Night Angel of St. Cuthbert\!" — while the rest of the party strolled after him at a safe ten feet. The air thickened into a dry, moistureless mist. The scurrying stopped. Baelson took a knee, rolled a 19 on survival, and learned the bitter truth: *the monster had never come this way at all.* Deceived again. "The fog of transgression will not save your soul," he informed the empty fog.

Vlix cast light on a rock and left it glowing purple on the ground — a Hansel-and-Gretel beacon — and Randælfine set dancing lights swirling chaotically above it, swaying along with his own arrhythmic shadows. Then the haze thinned, and the tunnel opened onto wonder.

A vast gorge, like the inside of a hollow mountain — the mist they'd walked through now hanging as a cloud-ceiling above them. Water dripped through it, and where each drop struck stone it burst in a small blue biological flash, trickling down slopes strewn with patches of bioluminescent fungus: a galaxy of little lights descending into the dark. And at the very bottom, built into the living rock, a small cathedral — flanked by two giant carved dwarves, its great door dimly lit by braziers.

> **Sister Maugra:** "Behold — the Vault of Dumathoin. And that is where Cororic is."

Randælfine's religion check painted the picture: hoarder-monks of the Underdark, xenophobic but not greedy, who see the gathering of treasures into the vault as a benevolent liberation. *("Oh,"* purred Randælfine, undulating every limb at a different frequency, *"so they could have eldritch things down there.")*

Vlix's sharp eyes found a second way down — a thin, treacherous ledge tracing the northern wall. ("A fairy-inspired *alleged ledge*," the table ruled, after the DM briefly forgot she wasn't a hunter.) Randælfine free-climbed a twenty-foot wall, hammering in five pitons for the rest; everyone made the climb. On the ledge, single file — Baelson at point with the torch, Vlix second from the rear, Randælfine trailing forty feet back in the flicker — they passed overflowing pools where impossible mushrooms grew heads out of heads, and orange lightning-bugs drifted over glowing water.

Then the ledge simply *ended*: a ten-foot gap of sheer wall, eighty feet above the gorge floor. Sister Maugra shouldered Baelson aside — *"Let us handle this. We know what to do"* — roped herself to Brother Hethrin, and anchored him as he made the traverse. He slipped once, four feet out, caught himself, and landed the far side.

A literal cliffhanger. The DM, having a World Cup final to attend, called it there.

## Session III — *Squeeze Out the Magic*

*July 26th*

The party crossed the rope one by one — Baelson muttering a prayer to St. Cuthbert, Vlix in heels, Randælfine slipping twice and *whee*\-ing both times — and pressed on along the ridge, one hundred twenty feet above a firelit pool.

Out of the shadows, silent as thought, dropped **Pim** — sticking the landing with a nineteen and a courteous bow. The guide who had parted ways with them in the tunnels had been shadowing the deep, and she came bearing intelligence: goblins at the pool below, and two **kuo-toa** hidden in an alcove ahead. The kuo-toa knew the party was there. The goblins did not.

> **Baelson:** "If the kuo-toa do not transgress the law of men, we will leave them unmolested. But the goblins — if they mean us harm, they will come to understand the righteous fury of St. Cuthbert. Lead on, Pim."

Pim, for her part, found herself intrigued by this loud human who *announces his intentions.* Nobody in the Underdark announces their intentions.

Randælfine had a better idea than diplomacy. Hinged at the waist, scuttling the shadow-line, he approached the hidden kuo-toa — who hissed first: *"Quiet. Don't move. Not for us. For you. Shout here and the whole gorge will hear us."* Their leader, **Glul**, was a cultist of unusual sanity, and Randælfine fed him a seventeen-deception masterpiece: the goblins below had stolen *great magic* from the party. Magic of the depths. And the goblins, you see, were *spongy* —

> **Randælfine:** "The best thing would be to leap down atop them from above and land upon them. That will squeeze the magic out of their spongy bodies and into you."

He set a voice echoing in each fish-man's skull — *squeeze out the magic and take it for us... squeeze out the magic and take it for us...* — while Vlix, inspired, added a catchy prestidigitation jingle underneath, like an ice cream truck of the damned. Glul spat that he hated "the drowned," coveted the magic, and sensibly declined to leap from lethal height — but he led them down toward the goblin camp, where six goblins dredged silt from a waist-deep pool under the eye of a shield-bearing chief and a cloth-robed shaman muttering over colored mushrooms by a fire. ("The workers," Pim's player observed, "do not control the means of production.")

The meeker kuo-toa, **Lib**, whipped into a frenzy by promises of baptism and salvation, made the twenty-foot leap onto goblin G5 — rolled a one — and belly-flopped headfirst into six inches of water. So began the Battle of the Silt Pool.

It was a rout with casualties. Pim's crossbow spoke first, a natural twenty into a goblin's neck that set the whole cavern screeching. Baelson waded in with shield raised and dodge held, scooping silt into the air with his shield to obscure his vengeance, drawing every arrow in the camp and eating none of them. Poor Lib grappled his goblin, was cut clean in half at the gut by a scimitar, and died the way he lived: enthusiastically. Vlix rained Eldritch Blasts from the high ledge — one goblin simply *popped*, sprinkling into the pool — and when the goblin chief closed to melee, she dropped a Witch Bolt of *nineteen damage* into him while he stood in a foot of water. The table convened its physics subcommittee, ruled the current dissipated in a ten-foot radius, and stripped every creature in the pool of its reactions. The chief, throat raw from screaming, called the surrender: *"Enough. Enough. Stop."*

Gri the Asht-Chewer, the shaman, poison-dusted the dwarves and fled into the dark. The rest broke or begged.

What followed was an interrogation of profound moral ambiguity. Baelson dunked the goblin leader **Nyx** headfirst into the silt, demanding the location of kuo-toa prisoners. ("Leave Nyx alone\!" wailed the last free goblin, before splashing off into the darkness — Baelson let him go: *"It's in their nature to flee in the face of absolute justice. A goblin in hand is worth two in the silt."*) Nyx protested that they were simple mushroom harvesters — *"All we want is the mushrooms\! You fishes are ridiculous\!"* — and, ransacked, gave up a pouch of yellow long-stemmed mushrooms, four gold pieces he insisted were his, and, in exchange for a freedom Randælfine promised with a deception roll of ten that Nyx *completely believed*, one final treasure:

A stone. Ordinary at a glance — but on one face, embedded rather than carved, the outline of an open eye. *"You hold it over your eye,"* Nyx said, *"and it lets you see things."*

Randælfine held it to his left eye like a monocle. The DM slid him a private message. When it was done, the drow announced two things: aloud, for the dwarves searching the camp — *"This stone is a fake. Nothing about it works."* And silently, into every party member's mind, one by one:

> **Randælfine** *(telepathically)*: "Don't trust the dwarves."

In the shadows away from the clerics, Vlix — her skin's blue flecks pulsing soothingly, her voice pure southern honey ("Hush now, honey... I am your friend") — charmed Nyx outright (the DM rolled a one). The friendly goblin spilled everything: the stone came in trade from a fish-person; it had worked only once for him, showing *someone smiling over his body right before he died* — at which Vlix cheerfully offered to cut his throat while smiling, just to close the loop. The silt, Nyx explained, was cooked by Gri into a drug called **brain cane**: *it makes you smarter, and talk a lot faster.*

Brother Hethrin returned from the campfire hauling ten three-pound pouches of white powder. The party looked at the drugs. The party looked at each other.

> **Vlix's player:** "Yeah, sorry Tony. We're going to stop here and instead set up a drug cartel. I know you clearly want us to go into the temple, but there's money to be made."

The DM, to his credit, rolled with it. Randælfine took a bag ("if this makes you smarter and jibber faster, he's *all for it*"). Vlix took a bag. Baelson declined, on the record, while formulating an "investigating the drug trade" rationale for the company he keeps. When Vlix passed the stone before her right eye and slowly lowered it, she said only: *"I see what you mean about the dwarves."*

Brother Hethrin healed the wounded — Randælfine's mental approval arriving as *"Yes. Deplete them of their magic"* — and the party settled by the fire for a short rest. It was Pim who noticed: **Sister Maugra was gone.** Walking alone down the gorge, ignoring them all. *"I could not stop her,"* said Brother Hethrin. *"She told me to stay, to see you succeed."*

They abandoned the rest and gathered their things. As they left camp, far below, Sister Maugra opened the great doors of the cathedral — and went inside.

*The party reached Level 2\. The one-shot was now, undeniably, a campaign.*

## Session IV — *Be Still and Be Seen*

*August 1st*

Newly leveled and terribly confident — Baelson with Protection style, Bless, Cure Wounds, and Divine Smite; Randælfine with at-will False Life (a half-minute of gibbering, gesturing, and sprinkled spirits, once an hour, forever) and the tentacled Arms of Hadar, re-flavored as the arms of Cthulhu; Vlix with a charisma-charged, *shoving* Eldritch Blast; Pim with Cunning Action — the party descended the gorge to the doors of the **Cathedral of Dumathoin**.

Pim scouted ahead and etched a message in the secret stone-script of the deep gnomes: her location, the hour, and the company she kept — *two tops, two bottoms. A balanced party.*

The doors were sixteen feet of ancient dwarven stonework, flanked by carved dwarves with maces and shields — and someone had recently, haphazardly, carved **new open eyes on top of the statues' eyes**. Brother Hethrin named it for what it was: defilement. To the Dumathoin, the eye is sacred; to deface one is a grave transgression. *"This church may not be what we want it to be,"* he said. *"But Cororic is inside."*

Baelson, having rolled a natural one looking for the missing child's tracks and worked himself into a frenzy over the lost trail, kicked the doors open.

The antechamber was dust, scattered pews — and a stench like nothing living. Not one death but *many*, layered across years and species, an ancient mixture of every flavor of rot. The walls crawled with dwarven carvings, saner near the door, madder toward the back: near the entrance, over and over, ***"It's not here. It's not here."*** Deeper in: ***"This is not the prize."*** Cororic, keeper of treasures, had gone mad searching for something the vault did not hold. Baelson, reading the despair of a man seeking and never finding, began — entirely by accident — to *vibe* with him. (The table broke into U2. It could not be helped.)

At the far doors, Randælfine pressed both palms to the stone, rolled a natural twenty on perception, and read the bouquet like a sommelier: dead drow, dead dwarves, dead gnomes, in every stage of decay. *"I have never smelled anything like this before. Oh — we will find something* totally *different in there. We should open it."*

First: preparations. Randælfine took a bump of brain cane from his three-pound bag and offered his pinky nail around. Pim partook — a drow-sized dose in a gnome-sized body — and became **Pimp**. Vlix partook. Baelson was already high on St. Cuthbert. Brother Hethrin politely abstained. Thus fortified, they opened the door.

The inner cathedral held two hundred dead, laid neatly in **concentric circles** — drow, dwarves, gnomes, humans, kuo-toa, rotted across decades, strange fungus blooming from the older rings. An open-air mass grave; an Underdark war crime. Above the exact center of it all floated **the eye**: eight feet up, lidless, ethereal, a black hole the mind slid off of, impossible to focus on. On the far side, five kuo-toa sang a droning, out-of-sync sine-wave chant, breathing in relay so the sound never broke, conducted by a priest-like figure with a spear for a baton — **Ulu**. Zombie-eyed thralls swayed among the corpses. And before the eye, standing pleasantly over the body of a small boy, was a smiling old dwarf.

**Cororic.** *"Welcome\! I didn't realize you would come too. It's wonderful."*

Randælfine's telepathy went to every skull in the party: *he is the one from the stone. He is a murderer. Don't trust him.* Into Baelson's head, plainer still — and Baelson, whose whole soul had narrowed to the child at Cororic's feet, charged straight across the room. Nobody stopped him. Nobody even flinched, which offended him slightly. Cororic only touched his shoulder, gently: *"The boy is almost done. Almost counted. There's no need for this level of aggression."*

It was Vlix who broke. Something about a man's hand reaching for a child, something older than her powers:

> **Vlix:** "Let's not get handsy, sugar." *(Eldritch Blast — 17 to hit, 10 damage, Cororic hurled ten feet back.)*

Surprise round. Initiative. **Battle in the boneyard.**

The fight sprawled across the corpse-rings — difficult terrain everywhere, bodies snapping and squelching underfoot, which Randælfine found *utterly delicious*. Baelson scooped up the entranced but living boy and fought his way clear through grappling thralls. Brother Hethrin — horrified, screaming *"No\!"* — turned on the party to defend his lost saint, and took Randælfine's rapier through the chainmail for twelve, delivered at full brain-cane speed: *"I'm going to skewer you and skewer your organs and skewer your eyes and skewer your kneecaps—"* Guiding Bolts flew from cleric to warlock; Vlix's celestial blood shrugged off radiant fire that would have killed a lesser level one, and she learned in that moment something of what she truly is.

Cororic barely fought. He *counted*. He pointed at Vlix and tolled the dead — she saved on a natural 20, and he tilted his head like a confused dog when her life force refused to leave her. He looked into Baelson's eyes — *"Fellow paladin, there is no need for this violence"* — and for the first time in his life, the Night Angel felt something he had no word for. Doubt. *Charmed.* ("We're potentially making him more dangerous," Benjamin noted. "Just pointing that out.") Baelson, unable to raise a hand against the tragic, fallen figure, said only: *"I understand why you abandoned the Dumathoin and gave way to this heresy."* He looked at Cororic and saw a man who sought and never found.

He looked at Randælfine and the drow simply *breakdanced* out of Cororic's *"Be still and be seen,"* moonwalking through a hold spell on a sixteen.

The choir advanced in waltz-time, one-two-three, hurling sticky nets — Baelson netted and restrained, Pim cartwheeling clear on a nineteen, Pimp taking a bow. Randælfine waded to the center of the acolytes and unfurled the **Arms of Cthulhu**, black tentacles lashing a ten-foot circle of ghoul-faced singers and turncoat cleric alike — and the moment he struck all the acolytes at once, something struck *back*. A vision seared his mind for four psychic damage, and with it a name: the choir is bound to the eye, and the eye belongs to **the Witness**. (The drow let the whiskey take that one.)

Vlix's Witch Bolt found Cororic for twenty-one points, flipping him legs-over-head — and the old dwarf, still smiling, still *pleasant*, spent his entire movement fleeing fifty feet to snap the lightning's tether, crying out to his acolytes: *"The eye is at hand\!"*

The session ended mid-battle, blades out, the choir still singing:

**Baelson 11/23 · Pim(p) 16/16 · Randælfine 10/11 · Vlix 9/15**

*The boy is safe behind Baelson's shield. The Witness has a name. And somewhere in the counting dark, it has not yet finished counting them.*
