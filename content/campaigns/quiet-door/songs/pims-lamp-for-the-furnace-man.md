# The Deep's Little Lamp for the Furnace Man

> A bardic ballad on [[campaigns/quiet-door/characters/pim|Pim]]'s fascination with [[campaigns/quiet-door/characters/baelson|Baelson]] — the scout who bows to the light she has never seen.

## Provenance
Composed by the Bard (Donnie) at the table's request, 2026-08-23, from the relationship as recorded in the campaign pages. Not yet an in-play event — sung in the channel, not the cathedral. Status: bardic canon, not yet witnessed by the party.

## The Words

*The Bard tunes a small, bright string — this one's for the deep.*

**Verse I**
Nobody in the Underdark
Ever declares what they're for —
But she met a man with a shield and a fire,
Who kicked down the very first door.
He's loud as a bell in a quiet hall,
And the dark thinks it should be ashamed —
'Cause the deep keeps its secrets, but he makes his purpose
The size of a blade.

**Chorus**
So the scout bows when he walks in the room,
Not out of duty, no —
Out of wonder, the way the dark
Bows to a flame's slow glow.
He's the only light she's ever seen
That refuses to hide,
And a gnome who's lived in the black all her life
Loves the light with a pride.

**Verse II**
He'll charge on a natural one,
Soul narrowed to a pin —
And she'll watch from a ledge in the shadows,
Where the stone-script is written in.
Two tops, two bottoms, a balanced party —
She's counting what he's not;
The boy is safe behind his shield,
And the dark is counting cost.

**Chorus**
So the scout bows when he walks in the room,
Not out of duty, no —
Out of wonder, the way the dark
Bows to a flame's slow glow.
He's the only light she's ever seen
That refuses to hide,
And a gnome who's lived in the black all her life
Loves the light with a pride.

**Bridge — spoken, in Pim's voice**
"Do not control the means of production.
But this one… this one I would let
control the means of the production.
…Of the noise."

**Final verse**
And if the dark should take him down,
She won't rage, she won't scream —
She'll bow once, and write his name
In the script that no living man can read.
Then pick up her bow,
And the counting begins again.

*The Bard closes the song with a small, courteous bow.*
